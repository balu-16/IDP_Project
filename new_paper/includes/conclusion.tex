We evaluated L6-guided Grover Adaptive Search for selecting compact evidence sets in a hybrid retrieval pipeline. Classical retrieval and a cross-encoder supply candidates and relevance scores, while amplitude amplification searches for improving document subsets in an ideal compact simulator. On the SciFact and NFCorpus follow-up tests, protected-prefix refinement preserved the first five results and reduced mean embedding redundancy relative to matched base selection. The recall changes and their uncertainty are reported explicitly; preserving neural-score thresholds does not prove preservation of judged relevance. The full FiQA evaluation uses the earlier configuration and extends domain and corpus-size coverage, without validating the refinement on that collection. Matched classical optimizers are essential controls: any shared effectiveness benefit belongs to the constrained selection policy rather than uniquely to Grover search. Additional neural scoring and simulation remain part of the complete pipeline cost. The evidence therefore supports a reproducible relevance--redundancy tradeoff, not physical quantum acceleration or monetary savings. Future work should complete the remaining configuration evaluations, implement reversible constraint oracles, and test quantum resources and generated-answer quality independently.
