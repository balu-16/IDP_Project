\begin{abstract}
Selecting useful retrieval evidence requires balancing relevance with repeated information. We evaluate L6-guided Grover Adaptive Search as document-set selection after classical dense and lexical retrieval. A cross-encoder supplies relevance scores, and a compact ideal simulator performs phase marking and amplitude amplification over improving subsets. The base policy selects eight of fifteen candidates. A constrained refinement expands the candidate pool, preserves the first five results, and searches for a less redundant three-document tail under neural-score constraints. Full tests cover scientific claims, nutrition-related retrieval, and financial questions. \resultinput{abstract_scope.tex} \resultinput{abstract_findings.tex} We provide paired query statistics, classical and neural controls, independent metric checks, oracle accounting, and mathematical preservation guarantees. These results characterize a relevance--redundancy tradeoff without establishing physical quantum acceleration, lower monetary cost, or improved generated answers.
\end{abstract}
\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Grover Adaptive Search, document retrieval, cross-encoder, evidence selection, redundancy.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
Retrieval-augmented systems retrieve documents before producing an answer \cite{lewis2020retrieval}. A limited evidence budget makes both relevance and overlap important: several near-duplicate passages can consume space that might otherwise cover a different aspect of a question. Independent document scores do not explicitly consider relationships among selected documents. Set selection instead evaluates an evidence bundle as a whole, but its benefit must be measured against strong relevance models and classical methods optimizing the same objective.

Grover search reduces oracle queries under a specified access model \cite{grover1996search,brassard2002amplitude}. It does not remove classical embedding, retrieval, state preparation, or reversible computation costs. In a threshold-marking reranker, equally marked documents have symmetric ideal amplification. Their measurement probabilities therefore do not themselves provide a graded relevance ordering. Transforming those probabilities into ranking scores requires controls for both threshold fallback and relevance distortion.

We retain that original policy as a legacy comparator and use Grover Adaptive Search (GAS) to search document subsets instead \cite{gilliam2021gas}. A neural cross-encoder supplies relevance evidence, while document embeddings measure redundancy. An earlier base policy jointly optimizes relevance and redundancy over fifteen candidates. The follow-up refinement protects its five highest-ranked outputs, expands the available tail candidates, and minimizes redundancy subject to neural-score constraints. Grover amplification affects the sampled subset; it is not substituted for a learned relevance score.

Our questions are: (1) can constrained selection reduce redundancy while preserving the initial ranking prefix and limiting observed recall loss; (2) how does it compare with matched greedy, exact, random, lexical, and neural methods; and (3) what simulation work and resource assumptions are required? The contribution is an evaluated retrieval formulation and reproducible accounting, rather than a new quantum search algorithm. Gains shared with classical optimizers support the selection policy, not a uniquely quantum benefit. Redundancy is an embedding-similarity proxy, not a duplicate percentage, judged coverage score, or answer-quality measure.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
Amplitude amplification separates oracle queries from the cost of constructing and executing each oracle \cite{grover1996search,brassard2002amplitude}. Search with an unknown marked-state count motivates randomized iteration schedules \cite{boyer1998tight}; quantum minimum finding updates incumbent thresholds \cite{durr1996minimum}. Gilliam, Woerner, and Gonciulea formulate GAS for constrained polynomial binary optimization and describe arithmetic oracles \cite{gilliam2021gas}. Our compact simulator reproduces ideal phase and reflection operations over a small subset basis. It does not realize all corresponding decision, arithmetic, and constraint registers on a quantum device.

Fixed-cardinality selection can be represented with fixed-weight bit strings. Dicke-state constructions show that such states need not always require arbitrary dense state synthesis \cite{bartschi2019dicke}. However, a preparation method's theoretical circuit bound cannot be assigned to an implementation that does not execute it. Data access, arithmetic, and constraint checks remain central to interpreting quantum claims \cite{montanaro2016quantum}.

Maximal marginal relevance (MMR) balances relevance with novelty relative to previously selected documents \cite{carbonell1998mmr}. Reciprocal-rank fusion (RRF) combines retrieval lists \cite{cormack2009rrf}, and BM25 supplies lexical relevance \cite{robertson2009bm25}. Exact and greedy controls distinguish an objective's value from a search mechanism's value. Our matched refinement controls share the expanded candidates, fixed prefix, score constraints, and redundancy definition.

Sentence encoders provide reusable dense vectors \cite{reimers2019sentencebert}. Cross-encoders jointly encode a query and passage to estimate relevance \cite{nogueira2019bert}. The common \texttt{all-MiniLM-L6-v2} embedding model and \texttt{ms-marco-MiniLM-L6-v2} cross-encoder have different roles and training objectives \cite{minilm_model,crossencoder_models}. ``MS MARCO'' identifies the latter's passage-ranking training task, rather than an alternative generic encoder. BEIR supplies heterogeneous collections for testing retrieval across domains \cite{thakur2021beir}.

\subsection{Development choices and follow-up protocol}
SciFact development is a deterministic 20\% subset of official training queries; NFCorpus and FiQA use official development splits. The base lexical grid is \(\beta\in\{0,0.25,0.5\}\), and its diversity grid is \(\lambda\in\{0,0.01,0.025,0.05\}\). All stochastic settings use seeds 0--4. Eligibility requires development mean nDCG@5 and Recall@8 to equal or exceed the same-shortlist L6-only baseline within \(10^{-12}\). Eligible settings prefer higher nDCG, higher recall, lower redundancy, smaller penalty, and smaller lexical weight, in that order. Zero penalty is retained when it wins.

L6 MMR is independently tuned over \(\{0,0.1,0.25,0.5,0.75,1\}\), prioritizing nDCG, recall, lower redundancy, and smaller weight. Its selected weight is zero on these collections. This is a relevance-prioritizing development choice, not the entire MMR tradeoff frontier. Cosine-guided controls retain separate frozen development settings. Legacy thresholds \(\{0.3,0.5,0.7\}\) are selected on development nDCG@5 using matched classical sampling; ties prefer 0.5, then 0.3, then 0.7.

\section{L6-Guided Grover Set Selection}\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Retrieval and base objective}
Let \(c_i\in[-1,1]\) be parent query cosine among dense top 64 and \(b_i\geq0\) its BM25 score. The reproduced hybrid score is
\begin{equation}
h_i=(1-\beta)\frac{1+c_i}{1+\max_jc_j}+\beta\frac{b_i}{\max_jb_j}.\label{eq:hybrid}
\end{equation}
If lexical scores are negligible, use only the dense term. If its denominator is negligible, use \((1+c_i)/2\). Negligibility uses tolerance \(10^{-12}\). Hybrid top fifteen is sorted by score and document ID. Keep raw logits \(z_i\), cosine, embeddings, and normalized relevance separate. Within the original fifteen,
\begin{equation}
r_i=\frac{z_i-\min_jz_j}{\max_jz_j-\min_jz_j},\label{eq:relevance}
\end{equation}
with \(r_i=0.5\) for negligible range. Negative logits are valid.

For normalized representative vectors \(v_i\), define \(d_{ij}=\operatorname{clip}(v_i^\top v_j,0,1)\). For \(|S|=k=8\),
\begin{align}
\red(S)&=\binom{k}{2}^{-1}\sum_{\{i,j\}\subset S}d_{ij},\label{eq:red}\\
U(S)&=k^{-1}\sum_{i\in S}r_i-\lambda\red(S).\label{eq:utility}
\end{align}
The base uses eight-bit coefficients, \(a=255\), and energy
\begin{align}
q_i&=\operatorname{round}(ar_i),\quad
q_{ij}=\operatorname{round}\!\left(\frac{2a\lambda}{k-1}d_{ij}\right),\\
E_b(S)&=-\sum_{i\in S}q_i+\sum_{\{i,j\}\subset S}q_{ij}.\label{eq:base_energy}
\end{align}
Rounding is ties-to-even. Raw-L6 top eight initializes the incumbent; raw L6 descending with document-ID ties determines final order. Summing coefficient errors gives, for each fixed set,
\begin{equation}
\left|\frac{-E_b(S)}{ak}-U(S)\right|\leq\frac{k+1}{4a}.\label{eq:base_bound}
\end{equation}
This bounds approximation, not retrieval accuracy. At zero penalty the top-L6 incumbent maximizes relevance and is retained; quantization does not justify arbitrary equal-energy reordering.

Score the original shortlist first and preserve its exact logits; score only additional candidates afterward. This prevents neural batching changes from altering the base result.

For each seed, let base ranking be \((s_1,\ldots,s_8)\), fixed prefix \(H=(s_1,\ldots,s_5)\), and original tail \(T_0\). Search \(T\subseteq C\setminus H\), \(|T|=3\). Ascending sorted tail logits must satisfy
\begin{equation}
z_{(j)}(T)\geq z_{(j)}(T_0)-\varepsilon,\quad j=1,2,3,\quad\varepsilon=10^{-12}.\label{eq:dominance}
\end{equation}
Candidates below the smallest original-tail logit minus \(\varepsilon\) can safely be discarded: they cannot occupy even the lowest position. Let \(M\) be the remaining tail-pool size. Constraint~\eqref{eq:dominance} is equivalent to requiring at least \(4-j\) tail scores above each fixed bound \(z_{(j)}(T_0)-\varepsilon\), using inclusive comparisons. A count violation identifies a sorted position below its bound, and meeting every count constraint places each position at or above its bound. This also handles tied scores.

Minimize \(\red(H\cup T)\), including prefix--prefix, prefix--tail, and tail--tail pairs. The fixed prefix term is constant but remains in reported redundancy. With \(a=2^{16}-1\), refinement energy is
\begin{equation}
E_r(H\cup T)=\sum_{\{i,j\}\subset H\cup T}\operatorname{round}(ad_{ij}).\label{eq:refine_energy}
\end{equation}
Mark valid states with strictly lower integer energy than the incumbent. Accept a measured update only if true redundancy also decreases by more than \(\varepsilon\). Output the original prefix followed by the new tail in raw-L6 order. This deliberately differs from a global L6 sort when an added document scores above a protected one.

\subsection{Guarantees and counterexample}
\textit{Prefix preservation.} With fixed judgments, nDCG@5 depends only on the first five IDs and their order. Preserving them keeps DCG@5 and its ideal denominator identical. Per-query nDCG@5 and its seed/query means are therefore unchanged by construction. This is not an empirical accuracy gain uniquely due to Grover search; nDCG@8 and Recall@8 can change.

\textit{Feasibility and redundancy.} The unchanged \(T_0\) satisfies~\eqref{eq:dominance}, survives the prefilter, and appears in the size-three basis. It supplies eight unique outputs even at budget exhaustion or when no improvement is sampled. Every accepted update strictly lowers true redundancy. Induction therefore gives final redundancy no greater than base redundancy. Strict improvement is not guaranteed per query; a trivial basis or an optimum can retain the original set.

\textit{Quantization bound.} Each of the 28 rounded pair coefficients differs from its unrounded value by at most \(1/2\). Thus
\begin{equation}
\left|\frac{E_r(S)}{28a}-\red(S)\right|\leq\frac{1}{2a}.\label{eq:refine_bound}
\end{equation}
Applying this twice bounds exact quantized-optimizer redundancy regret by \(1/a\) relative to the exact float optimizer over the same admissible domain. It does not prove budget-limited GAS reaches that optimizer. At sixteen bits the per-set bound is approximately \(7.63\times10^{-6}\); model error is not bounded by coefficient precision.

\textit{Recall counterexample.} Replacing a relevant original-tail document by an irrelevant but higher-L6-scoring document can satisfy~\eqref{eq:dominance} while reducing recall. Therefore score dominance does not guarantee true relevance. Empirical recall gates and paired uncertainty remain necessary; neither an unchanged prefix nor higher logits establish overall noninferiority.

\subsection{Amplitude amplification and budgets}
The base has \(\binom{15}{8}=6{,}435\) cardinality-feasible subsets. Refinement has \(F=\binom{M}{3}\) size-three subsets after score prefiltering, including subsets later failing~\eqref{eq:dominance}. Only valid improving states are marked. For \(|u\rangle=F^{-1/2}\sum_x|x\rangle\), one step is
\begin{align}
G&=(2|u\rangle\langle u|-I)O_\tau,\\
O_\tau|x\rangle&=(-1)^{[\mathrm{valid}(x)\land E(x)<\tau]}|x\rangle.\label{eq:grover}
\end{align}
The simulator explicitly flips marked amplitudes and reflects amplitudes about their post-oracle mean. These operations preserve the norm; measurement samples squared amplitudes. This is ideal amplitude amplification executed classically, not execution on quantum hardware.

Each stage begins with its incumbent and window \(w=1\). A trial draws an integer from zero through \(\lceil w\rceil-1\), limited by remaining phase calls, prepares the uniform state, applies that many steps, and measures. Strict improvement resets \(w\); otherwise it grows by 1.2, capped at \(\sqrt F\). Each stage allows 64 phase calls, 32 trials, or a cooperative 15-second limit per seed. Baseline and refinement budgets are separate: a refined request can use up to 128 phase calls. The controller receives no optimum or marked-state count. Trivial bases and every termination reason remain recorded.

\section{Limitations and Reproducibility}\label{sec:limits}
The classical top-64 and representative-chunk policies can exclude useful evidence before selection. Expansion stays within that pool; neural scoring supplies the relevance signal. A fixed quantum pool does not demonstrate quantum corpus search. The L2/L6 controls are concrete neural baselines, not every modern reranker. Development-selected zero-diversity MMR does not establish superiority over its full operating curve.

Protected nDCG@5 is relative to a particular baseline prefix, not a globally optimal ranking. The recall gate is empirical, individual queries can worsen, and the configuration coverage differs as stated in Table~\ref{tab:headline}. Test reuse and the confirmation exception limit confirmatory inference. Embedding similarity does not directly measure semantic duplication, complementary evidence, hallucination, or answer quality.

The simulator is noiseless and exhaustively prepares objectives. Efficient reversible data loading, a full constrained oracle, a noise study, monetary/energy models, and generated-answer experiments are not demonstrated. Logical estimates exclude some workspace. Shared-host variation and different candidate pools further limit speed comparisons. Small amplitude storage is not total-system memory saving.

Selected runs retain query IDs, model revisions, data hashes, source snapshots, configurations, features, logits, timings, per-seed traces, sessions, and artifact hashes. A manuscript-only exporter verifies inputs, independently recomputes metrics from saved rankings, and generates result tables and outcome-bearing text. NIST checks and tie-corrected statistics remain bound to their original manifests, without rewriting experiments. A central mapping permits replacing a completed full-test source after coverage/provenance verification. Companion records retain all controls, protocol exceptions, commands, and outstanding limitations.
