Dear Editor,

Please consider the manuscript “Grover-Inspired Evidence Selection for Document Retrieval: Relevance Constraints, Failure Analysis, and Resource Accounting” as a Research Article for IEEE Access.

The manuscript examines constrained evidence-set selection after classical dense and lexical retrieval. It separates properties of a relevance–redundancy formulation from the choice of optimizer and from the cost of a complete neural retrieval pipeline. Its empirical basis comprises the completed SciFact, NFCorpus, and FiQA studies, with their recorded policy distinctions and negative findings retained.

This article extends our accepted ICECMSN conference manuscript, “Qubit Chat: A Controlled Evaluation of Grover-Inspired Reranking for Document Retrieval,” which is cited in the paper and awaits publication. The conference results, selection algorithms, mathematical guarantees, and native resource study are identified as prior work. The journal extension adds candidate-pool recall ceilings, query-level gain/loss distributions and failure cases, output stability across seeds, additive request-cost attribution, and a fuller synthesis of the measured resource evidence. No new benchmark rankings or neural inference were collected for this extension, and no results are represented as new experimental collection.

The article reports that matched classical refiners reach the same aggregate quality in the protected setting. It discloses recall losses, test reuse, the NFCorpus confirmation exception, and the failed FiQA refinement follow-up. It makes no claim of physical quantum speedup or improved generated answers. A reproducibility package accompanies the manuscript.

Before use, the authors must confirm the final submission declarations, author approval, funding/conflict statements, and compliance with the publisher's similarity requirements. This draft does not certify those facts on their behalf.
