\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
\subsection{Relevance estimation and evidence-set selection}
The pool presented to a selector depends on the retrieval signal. BM25 matches terms~\cite{robertson2009bm25}, whereas sentence encoders provide reusable query and document vectors~\cite{reimers2019sentencebert}. Reciprocal-rank fusion (RRF) combines rankings without requiring their scores to share a calibrated scale~\cite{cormack2009rrf}. We use these approaches to construct candidates and as classical comparisons; their costs are included in complete request time.

Cross-encoders process the query and passage jointly, rather than comparing independent vector encodings~\cite{devlin2019bert,nogueira2019bert}. Our embedding encoder and cross-encoder both belong to the MiniLM family~\cite{wang2020minilm}, but the former supplies normalized vectors and the latter produces query--passage logits after MS MARCO passage-ranking training~\cite{bajaj2016msmarco,minilm_model,crossencoder_models}. Those logits are neither calibrated relevance probabilities nor judgment labels. Higher logits therefore need not improve judged recall.

The broader ranking literature includes dense passage retrieval~\cite{karpukhin2020dpr}, late interaction~\cite{khattab2020colbert}, and sequence-to-sequence scoring~\cite{nogueira2020seq2seq}. Generative ranking agents extend the alternatives~\cite{sun2023rankgpt}, while comparative reranker studies examine architecture, training overlap, generalization, and cost~\cite{abdallah2025rerankers}. These are candidates for future controls, not results measured in our experiment. Our neural comparisons are limited to the pinned L2 and L6 cross-encoders on the declared pools.

RAG evaluation can separate indexing, retrieval, reranking, similarity, generation quality, and system cost~\cite{elkiran2026evaluation}. We make a similar distinction: our saved experiments concern retrieved evidence and selection resources, not generated-answer quality, which would require its own evaluation.

Set-selection methods also differ in what they compare. Maximal marginal relevance (MMR) selects documents sequentially, balancing relevance against similarity to earlier choices~\cite{carbonell1998mmr}; our objective uses average pair similarity over a complete fixed-size set. Determinantal point processes provide a probabilistic model of diverse subsets~\cite{kulesza2012dpp}. They are context here, not an evaluated baseline. \subsection{Quantum search and reversible resource accounting}
Amplitude amplification increases the probability of marked states; it does not rank equally marked documents by relevance~\cite{grover1996search,brassard2002amplitude}. Randomized schedules address an unknown marked-state count~\cite{boyer1998tight}, and minimum finding updates an incumbent threshold~\cite{durr1996minimum}. GAS applies these ideas to constrained polynomial binary optimization~\cite{gilliam2021gas}. Our subset objective uses that framework to represent interactions among selected documents.

Work on GAS considers both schedule efficiency and constants hidden by asymptotic query complexity~\cite{ominato2024gas}, as well as initialization, rotations, and gate construction in particular applications~\cite{norimoto2024gas}. An oracle call has a cost determined by its encoded task. Our measured oracle performs cardinality and tail-score checks, computes signed improvement, and uncomputes; the compact simulator uses another representation and cost boundary.

Structured fixed-weight preparation is possible, as deterministic Dicke-state constructions demonstrate~\cite{bartschi2019dicke}. It is not, however, the measured cost of software using generic preparation. Matrix-product-state simulation depends on entanglement~\cite{vidal2003simulation}; success on a small circuit does not establish feasibility for a larger one. Access models and implementation assumptions also matter when interpreting quantum algorithms~\cite{montanaro2016quantum}. We keep those boundaries in the resource analysis.

\subsection{Evaluation practice and extension over prior work}
BEIR provides heterogeneous collections and standardized retrieval tasks~\cite{thakur2021beir}. Our selected collections address scientific claims, nutrition-related search, and financial questions through SciFact, NFCorpus, and FiQA, respectively~\cite{wadden2020scifact,boteva2016nfcorpus,maia2018fiqa}. Their judgment distributions differ. Candidate coverage and the number of relevant documents per query therefore matter when interpreting absolute recall; we examine coverage within each collection.

We use gain-based ranking measures and independent implementations to support consistent evaluation~\cite{jarvelin2002gain,trec_eval}. For paired inference, queries are the observations, and the family of method comparisons must be considered~\cite{smucker2007tests,holm1979multiple,dror2018harnessing}. The recorded statistical families and earlier hypotheses remain intact; the added diagnostics are descriptive. Relative to the conference manuscript~\cite{conference2026}, the new empirical material analyzes saved outcomes rather than adding test collections or a newly tuned policy.

\subsection{Application-specific decision pipelines}
Related decision pipelines have been studied in other applications. Blockchain-mining work covers trust scoring \cite{puppala2023trust,puppala2023cluster}, VIKOR-based ranking \cite{puppala2024vikor}, reputation and consensus \cite{puppala2024reputation,puppala2025consensus}, and miner selection or classification \cite{puppala2024smnst,puppala2024svm}. Other studies address multimodal waste detection \cite{puppala2025marine}, smart-contract repair \cite{sravanthi2026incident}, financial-fraud detection \cite{sravanthi2025fraud}, plant-disease recognition \cite{sravanthi2025plant}, traffic forecasting \cite{sravanthi2025traffic}, and obscured-fruit classification \cite{sravanthi2026harvest}. We cite them as application context, not as results from our retrieval evaluation.
