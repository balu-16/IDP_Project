\section{Discussion, Limitations, and Reproducibility}\label{sec:discussion}
\subsection{What the combined evidence establishes}
The protected head explains part of the outcome by construction: a fixed prefix preserves the corresponding truncated ranking metric. What happens in the tail is empirical. Pair similarity falls on many queries, but relevance can change when candidate availability or neural scores are unfavorable. The coverage and loss analyses show why the guarantee and the losses can coexist.

The matched greedy and exact selectors reach the same reported aggregate quality as GAS. They share its candidate pool, objective, and constraints, so the gain over base selection cannot be attributed to amplitude amplification alone. The constrained domains are small enough to make exact controls practical, allowing that distinction to be tested.

The request-cost breakdown places a practical limit on the efficiency claim. Selector arithmetic occupies only a small part of the recorded pipeline; neural relevance inference takes a substantial share. Fewer phase queries alone cannot be assumed to produce a comparable end-to-end latency reduction when candidate scoring and upstream work remain. This conclusion concerns the recorded pipeline and host; a substantially different access model would need its own measurements.

\subsection{Construct and measurement validity}
The redundancy measure averages clipped embedding similarities. It does not directly measure repeated facts, contradiction, complementary evidence, or answer faithfulness. Necessary evidence can be correlated and incur a high penalty; irrelevant text can be dissimilar and incur a low one. An application-level study is needed to determine whether reducing this proxy helps the intended task.

Judgments concern parent documents, but relevance inference sees a representative passage. Supporting information may lie outside the parent's highest-cosine chunk, and the passage score may not describe the whole document. Dense top-64 filtering, hybrid top-fifteen filtering, and score-dominance constraints can each remove useful candidates. The judgment-based ceilings expose some losses; incomplete judgments mean they are not ceilings on all human relevance.

Preserving nDCG@5 does not settle the quality of the full set. Recall@8 and nDCG@8 may change, individual queries may lose substantial recall, and a downstream consumer may use the lower-ranked tail. The descriptive mean-retention gate also provides less evidence than a prospective statistical noninferiority design. These failures qualify the reported gains.

\subsection{Internal and external validity}
Because the official tests were reused during development, their earlier observation limits a confirmatory interpretation even though the recorded follow-up settings were not retuned on those outcomes. We report the failed NFCorpus training confirmation and the decision to continue. The failed FiQA refinement follow-up likewise prevents a claim of cross-domain success for the protected policy based on the earlier base row. The diagnostics and extreme examples are retrospective.

SciFact, NFCorpus, and FiQA cover different tasks, but not every domain, language, query distribution, or private collection. The measured L2/L6 controls do not exhaust modern rerankers. Training-data overlap and domain mismatch may affect the comparison, and a model-family name does not establish exact training/test disjointness. Prospective benchmarks and stronger rerankers are needed to assess generalization.

The latency measurements come from separate sessions on a shared host. The composition model charges applicable upstream costs to each policy but does not measure concurrency or production throughput. Warm-up and restoration are reported separately. Monetary cost, energy, quality of service, and physical quantum runtime were not measured. Translating unrestricted logical gate counts to device performance would require assumptions about routing, errors, and fault tolerance.

\subsection{Artifact and numerical reproducibility}
The journal artifact provides the LaTeX source, tables, figures, bibliography, analysis code, query diagnostics, seed metrics, and request timing components. Source mappings identify completed runs by manifest and evaluation hashes. Export reads those runs without collecting data, selecting documents, or performing neural inference; the journal scripts write within their own artifact directory.

Verification checks test-query, method, and seed coverage; source, model, and data identities; original logits; output cardinality; protected prefixes; and recorded feasibility. It independently recomputes 119,124 query--method--metric values from saved rankings and retains the experiment-bound NIST metric checks. Coverage diagnostics check the sizes and containment of dense, hybrid, and union candidate sets. A separate request-level check verifies the additive latency identity before the stacked mean figure is drawn.

The source collections and model records are public and cited, while the saved experimental artifacts remain local inputs without a public archival DOI. The journal's evidence exports make the present claims inspectable. Depositing the complete experiments would further support independent reproduction of the underlying runs.

\subsection{Future research}
The refined policy needs a prospective test on an untouched domain with relevance and loss criteria specified in advance. Such a study should retain failed outcomes, compare modern relevance models, and examine whether a full MMR tradeoff curve or another subset-diversity model offers a preferable operating point. A new generated-answer evaluation would be needed to connect changes in evidence sets to factuality, completeness, and answer usefulness.

Quantum implementation calls for further work on structured fixed-weight preparation, reversible coefficient access, hardware routing, noise, and fault-tolerant resource estimates. Comparisons must include classical algorithms solving the same admissible objective and the complete neural retrieval pipeline. Lower logical phase counts alone do not resolve the cost bottlenecks measured here.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
We evaluated Grover-inspired evidence selection under explicit relevance, candidate, and resource constraints. The completed three-collection evidence shows that a protected five-document prefix can accompany both lower pairwise embedding similarity and losses in judged recall. The journal extension examines candidate-pool ceilings, individual-query outcomes, seed stability, and additive request costs without a new benchmark or a retuned policy. Matched classical optimizers reach the same reported aggregate quality on the protected tests, while original and additional cross-encoder inference dominate composed online costs. The separately completed reversible-oracle study reports growing logical resources and limited simulation coverage; it does not establish physical-device acceleration. These findings bound the relevance--redundancy formulation and provide a reproducible account of its behavior. Claims about generalization, generated answers, or quantum advantage require prospective effectiveness tests and independently measured implementation costs. The guaranteed prefix, empirical tail quality, and negative outcomes must remain distinct in that assessment.
